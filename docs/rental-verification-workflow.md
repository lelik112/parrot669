# PARROT 669 — Rental Verification Workflow v0

Internal operating document. This is the first deliberately narrow service: factual verification of a rental opportunity in Barcelona before the client transfers money or signs.

## 1. What the client is buying

A structured factual check of a rental opportunity.

PARROT 669 does **not** label a property or person "safe", does not guarantee that fraud is impossible, and does not provide a legal opinion in this workflow.

The deliverable is an evidence-based report showing:

- what was confirmed;
- what could not be confirmed;
- what is inconsistent;
- what evidence was reviewed;
- what still requires the client's decision or specialist advice.

## 2. Intake

Minimum information requested from the client:

- listing URL **and** a saved copy (screenshots/PDF/export) captured at intake;
- property address, if known;
- name and contact details of the person offering the property;
- proposed rent, deposit, reservation payment or other requested payment;
- draft contract, if available;
- identity/property documents already received by the client;
- payment instructions already received;
- client's deadline;
- client's main concern.

Optional:

- video/photos from previous conversations;
- email or chat screenshots;
- agency name;
- cadastral/reference information;
- any specific claim the client wants checked.

## 3. Scope selection

The case is split into modules.

### A. Remote verification

Checks that can be performed without visiting the property.

Typical tasks:

- inspect the listing and supplied materials;
- compare names, addresses, dates and property details across documents;
- check whether the property can be identified consistently;
- check public or legitimately obtainable records where appropriate;
- compare the identity/role claimed by the person offering the property with the evidence supplied;
- review the payment request for inconsistencies;
- identify claims that remain unsupported.

### B. Physical visit

Only when useful and agreed.

Typical tasks:

- confirm that the address/property exists;
- confirm access to the building/property when possible;
- capture timestamped photos/video;
- compare visible condition/layout with the listing;
- verify visible details requested by the client;
- document anything materially inconsistent.

A visit is evidence collection, not a structural, legal or technical inspection.

### C. Third-party confirmation

Where appropriate and with a lawful basis:

- request clarification from the person offering the property;
- request missing supporting evidence;
- confirm a stated relationship to an agency/company/property;
- document refusal, non-response or contradictions.

## 4. Evidence handling

Every relevant item gets an evidence ID. Public listing pages must be snapshotted at intake because links can become unavailable, change, or block automated access.

Example:

- E01 — Idealista listing PDF/screenshot
- E02 — Draft rental agreement
- E03 — ID document supplied by landlord
- E04 — Property record / public record extract
- E05 — Client chat screenshots
- E06 — Visit photos
- E07 — Written reply from agency

Never treat "we were told" as equivalent to documentary confirmation.

## 5. Status vocabulary

Use only these operational statuses:

- **CONFIRMED** — supported by adequate evidence reviewed by PARROT 669.
- **NOT CONFIRMED** — evidence is absent or insufficient.
- **INCONSISTENT** — reviewed sources materially conflict.
- **NOT CHECKED** — outside agreed scope or could not reasonably be checked.

Avoid verdicts such as "safe", "legit", "fraud", "trustworthy" unless reporting a verified external fact or authority statement.

## 6. Core verification questions

For each case, answer only what evidence supports:

1. Can the property be consistently identified?
2. Does the listing information match the address/property evidence?
3. Who is asking the client for money?
4. What role does that person claim to have?
5. What evidence supports that role?
6. Does the draft contract identify the same property and parties?
7. Do payment recipient details match the stated parties or explanation?
8. Are there contradictions in names, dates, addresses, amounts or documents?
9. Was the property physically visited?
10. What important claims remain unverified?

## 7. Stop / escalate conditions

Pause the case and flag it internally if:

- a document appears altered or identity manipulation is suspected;
- the client asks PARROT 669 to hold or transfer money;
- the requested work becomes legal representation, formal legal advice, brokerage/negotiation, technical inspection or another regulated/specialist activity;
- obtaining evidence would require impersonation, deception or unauthorized access;
- there is an immediate safety concern during a visit.

The report should then state what was not checked and why.

## 8. Deliverable

The client receives a concise report containing:

1. Case summary
2. Scope performed
3. Findings table
4. Material inconsistencies / red flags
5. Evidence reviewed
6. Items not confirmed
7. Suggested next factual checks
8. Limitations

The report should be useful in under five minutes. Annexes can hold photos and source detail.

## 9. Internal timing log

For the first 10 cases, record actual minutes spent on:

- intake;
- desk research;
- communication;
- travel;
- on-site work;
- report writing;
- follow-up.

Do not set long-term pricing from intuition. Use these logs to learn the true cost of the service.

## 10. Definition of done

A case is complete when:

- all agreed checks have a status;
- every material finding points to evidence;
- unsupported claims are explicitly marked;
- contradictions are visible, not buried in prose;
- the report contains no guarantee language;
- the client can see what remains unknown.
